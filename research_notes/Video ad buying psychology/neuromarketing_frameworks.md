# Neuromarketing and Creative-Effectiveness Frameworks (Industry Research Firms and Ad Platforms)

Conflict-of-interest note that applies to the whole file: nearly every source below is a vendor (Google, Meta, TikTok sell media; System1, Kantar, Ipsos, Nielsen sell ad-testing products). Their studies are mostly correlational, use their own proprietary predictive metrics as the outcome (not audited sales), and are not peer reviewed. The only peer-reviewed items here are in the neuromarketing-methods section.

## Google/YouTube ABCD framework

### Takeaway
ABCD (Attract/Attention, Brand, Connect, Direct) is a checklist of YouTube creative practices: hook immediately, brand early and often (audio as well as visual), make people feel or think something, and end with a clear call to action. Google claims a 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and a 17% lift in long-term brand contribution, but this was measured by Kantar's AI model *predicting* outcomes, not by observed sales.

### Cited Findings
- Attention: open with action or close-ups, use audio (music, voice-over, sound effects), and use bold imagery, strong colour contrast and bright visuals at the start. — [Think with Google / business.google.com](https://business.google.com/in/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/)
- Branding: "brand early, often, and richly", through product shots, pack shots, in-situ branding, graphics and voice-over. Google states "YouTube is almost entirely a sound-on experience" and recommends audio and musical brand cues. — [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/in/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/)
- Connection: educate, inspire or entertain; humanise the story; use humour or surprise; keep the message focused and avoid overcomplexity. — [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/in/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/)
- Direction: give a clear, simple next step using written CTAs, graphics, audio or narrative. — [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/in/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/)
- The claim: "On average, the ABCDs deliver a 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and a 17% lift in long-term brand contribution." Google's footnote cites "Google/Kantar Link AI, Global, 2021" and also says the research was "conducted with Ipsos, validated by independent reviewers Nielsen and Kantar". — [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/in/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/)
- Method (Kantar's account): Kantar scored about 11,000 ads through Link AI in one month and tested 180 creative features against *predicted* short-term sales and long-term brand equity, using a custom model and correlation analysis of ABCD compliance against those predicted outcomes. Kantar notes that survey research at this scale "would have been cost prohibitive and taken over a year". — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence)

### Inferences
- The 30%/17% figures are model-on-model. Kantar's Link AI predicts its own survey metrics, and those metrics are what is validated against sales. Treat the uplifts as directional, not as causal sales effects. Google, the media seller, commissioned the work.
- For short video: hook in the first seconds, show the brand early (visually and in audio), use one focused emotional or humorous idea, and end with a single explicit CTA. This is consistent with the Meta and Kantar findings below.

### Gaps
- The public material does not break down which individual ABCD guidelines contributed most to the uplift, or give confidence intervals. The footnote also conflicts (Kantar Link AI vs "with Ipsos, validated by Nielsen and Kantar"). I could not find the full technical report.

## System1 Group: Star/Spike ratings, The Extraordinary, fluent devices, Lemon / Look Out

### Takeaway
System1 argues that emotional intensity, measured by online face/emotion response, plus strong, consistent branding predicts effectiveness. Fluent devices (recurring brand characters or scenarios) and "right-brain" craft (sense of place, characters, dialogue, melody, humour) score best. Long-term effectiveness (Stars) is driven by positive emotion; short-term effect (Spike) by emotional intensity combined with fast brand recognition.

### Cited Findings
- Star Rating (1 to 5.9 Stars) predicts long-term brand growth effect. Spike Rating (around 1.0 to 1.33+) predicts short-term sales effect over the 8–10 weeks after airing and is derived from the strength of branding plus the intensity of emotional response. — [VideoWeek](https://videoweek.com/2026/01/14/the-spike-is-right-putting-the-guesswork-back-into-advertising/); [System1 blog](https://system1group.com/blog/5-game-changing-creative-tips-for-your-100-yard-super-bowl-countdown)
- "The Extraordinary Cost of Dull": System1 says the real enemy is neutrality. If viewers feel nothing they don't act, and more emotionally intense ads are more likely to prompt immediate action. — [System1 via search summary](https://system1group.com/blog/super-bowl-ads-with-greatest-sales-potential)
- Fluent devices / brand characters drive brand recognition when used consistently over time and are also emotive. In System1's Super Bowl analysis, brand-character ads averaged a 1.38 Spike vs 1.24 for celebrity ads, ahead of celebrity, music, sports and non-celebrity ads. — [System1 blog](https://system1group.com/blog/5-game-changing-creative-tips-for-your-100-yard-super-bowl-countdown)
- Lemon / Look Out (Orlando Wood, IPA). Drawing on Iain McGilchrist, right-brain features capture broad-beam attention and emotion. Left-brain features ("including close-up product shots and the stare") are associated with ads that push people away. Since 1992, right-brain features have declined and left-brain features have risen; UK ads average 3.9 left-brain vs 2.3 right-brain features. The fewer right-brain features an ad has, the less likely it is to get a high Star rating. — [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/are-ads-becoming-too-far-left-brain); [System1 Look Out page](https://system1group.com/look-out)
- Wood's left-brain features: flatness; abstracted product, feature or ingredient; abstracted body parts (hands, eyes, mouth, face); words obtruding on screen; voiceover; monologue/testimonial; adjectives used as nouns; freeze-frame; audio repetition (metered prose, sound effects); high rhythmic soundtrack. — [Direct New Ideas](https://www.directnewideas.com/magazine/watch_out_advertising_is_becoming_increasingly_left_brained_/?id=3409)
- Wood's right-brain features: a clear sense of place; one scene unfolding with progression; characters with agency; implicit, unspoken communication (knowing glances); dialogue; distinctive accents; wordplay; set in the past; reference to cultural works (pastiche/parody); music with a discernible melody. — [Direct New Ideas](https://www.directnewideas.com/magazine/watch_out_advertising_is_becoming_increasingly_left_brained_/?id=3409)
- Look Out is positioned as the playbook for rebuilding lost effectiveness through "broad-beam" attention, cultural vitality and an outward-looking perspective. — [System1](https://system1group.com/look-out)

### Inferences
- Several left-brain features are standard short-form performance-ad tropes: text on screen, rhythmic soundtrack, voiceover, product close-ups, direct-to-camera monologue. Wood's work suggests these may help sound-off comprehension and direct response but hurt long-term emotional brand building. That is a real tension with Meta/TikTok guidance (captions, text overlays, talking-head creators). Different outcome metrics probably explain it: platforms optimise recall and clicks, System1 optimises emotion-predicted brand growth.
- A recurring character or scenario (a fluent device) is a cheap way for a short-video brand to build both recognition and emotion across many cuts.
- Conflict of interest: System1 sells the test that produces Stars and Spikes, and the left/right-brain coding relies on System1's own classification. The neuroscience basis (McGilchrist's hemisphere theory) is contested as literal neuroscience and is best read as a metaphor for attention styles.

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve the primary Lemon/Look Out datasets (the IPA Databank linkage and sample sizes). The Look Out PDF was not machine-readable. Published validation of Star/Spike ratings against independent sales data was not found in this pass. The "Extraordinary" quote comes via a search summary, not a primary page I fetched.

## Nielsen: creative's share of sales contribution, and consumer-neuroscience findings

### Takeaway
Nielsen's 2017 analysis of about 500 CPG campaigns attributed 47% of advertising-driven sales lift to creative quality (49% including brand), more than reach (22%), targeting (9%) or recency (5%). Its neuroscience lab (EEG, eye tracking, facial coding, biometrics) found that most 30-second ads can be compressed to 15 seconds without loss, and that brand moments can be missed when characters steal attention.

### Cited Findings
- Creative accounts for 47% of an ad's sales impact. The analysis covers almost 500 CPG campaigns from 2016 to Q1 2017 across major media platforms, with 49% of sales lift attributed to creative (47% quality/messaging plus brand). Reach accounted for 22%, targeting 9% and recency 5%. — [Media in Canada](https://mediaincanada.com/?p=157079); [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/television-80662)
- Trend: the 2006 "Apollo Project" put creative at 65% of sales lift. Media's share rose from about 15% to 36% a decade later. — [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/television-80662)
- Nielsen Consumer Neuroscience combined EEG, facial coding, eye tracking and biometrics with self-report. EEG tracks moment by moment when an ad activates memory, attention or emotion. About 90% of neuroscience-optimised 15-second ads tested as well as their 30-second versions, and a majority tested better. — [Nielsen 2014](https://www.nielsen.com/id/insights/2014/the-argument-for-more-effective-short-form-ads)
- Brand placement: when eye tracking was overlaid on EEG, viewers looked at characters rather than the brand during the final branding moment. This was the basis for repositioning brand cues. — [Nielsen case study PDF](https://develop.nielsen.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2019/04/case-study-optimizing-creative-using-consumer-neuroscience.pdf)
- New Balance used Nielsen's compression algorithm, based on brain-wave data, to cut its 30-second NYC Marathon spot down. — [Adweek](https://develop.adweek.com/?p=1003571)

### Inferences
- For short video: build the brand into the moment of peak attention (on or next to the face or action viewers are already watching) rather than an end card that competes with it. Neuro-compression evidence supports cutting to the highest-engagement moments.
- The 47% figure is often quoted out of context. It is CPG only, measures *advertising-driven* sales lift (not total sales), and dates from 2016–17. It says creative matters relative to media factors, not that creative drives half of sales.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Nielsen's original 2017 release (the secondary sources returned 403 or summaries). Specific Nielsen neuroscience guidance on cut pacing and on faces (for example faces-to-camera effects) was not found in primary form. Nielsen's neuroscience unit was reportedly restructured, and I could not confirm current findings from 2019 onward.

## Ipsos: creative effectiveness, brand attribution and branded attention

### Takeaway
Ipsos frames effectiveness around "branded attention": capturing attention *and* encoding it to the brand. It measures this with Creative|Spark, which includes in-context attention, facial coding and a sales-validated Creative Effect Index. The public detail available is thin and largely product marketing.

### Cited Findings
- Creative|Spark Digital evaluates creative's power to "capture and retain Branded Attention" in a live-platform context, in a distracted environment. — [Ipsos Digital](https://www.ipsos.digital/creative-digital)
- Metrics: a Creative Effect Index (short-term behaviour-change potential), an Equity Effect Index (long-term choice), and Retained Branded Recognition (attention captured and encoded as branded memory). Facial coding is standard, and the index is described as "sales-validated". — [Ipsos Creative Assessment](https://www.ipsos.com/en-gh/creative-assessment)
- WARC (August 2023) reported a "new study shows a clear link between attention and brand effects". I could not access the body to confirm authorship or numbers. — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/feed/new-study-shows-a-clear-link-between-attention-and-brand-effects/en-gb/8527)
- Google's ABCD footnote says the ABCD research was "conducted with Ipsos". — [Think with Google](https://business.google.com/in/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/)

### Inferences
- The actionable principle shared across Ipsos, Nielsen and System1 is that attention without brand linkage is wasted. Brand cues should be integrated into the attention-grabbing content, not separated from it.

### Gaps
- I did not find primary Ipsos numbers on the drivers of brand attribution (for example the share of ads that are misattributed, or the effect of early branding or distinctive assets). The specific "branded attention" study figures could not be retrieved. Treat Ipsos as under-sourced in these notes.

## Meta creative best practices: mobile-first, sound-off, first 3 seconds, vertical

### Takeaway
Meta's core evidence is a 2017 Facebook × MetrixLab study of 759 ads. Mobile-first ads (brand in the opening seconds, about 20 seconds long, designed to work with sound off) beat TV ads adapted for mobile on recall. Popular multiplier figures (1.9x ROI, 4.8x for sound-off) and "85% watch without sound" circulate mainly through aggregators and are older.

### Cited Findings
- Facebook and MetrixLab (published December 2017) studied 759 video ads from more than 300 brands across 9 verticals and 25 countries, measuring aided and unaided brand recall, message recall, purchase intent and related metrics. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebooks-published-a-new-study-which-highlights-effective-mobile-video-te/512753); [MetrixLab](https://metrixlab.com/?p=15225)
- Only 38% of traditional narrative ads showed the brand in the opening seconds. Ads with strong brand presence had significantly higher aided recall than those with weak branding such as watermarks. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebooks-published-a-new-study-which-highlights-effective-mobile-video-te/512753)
- Mobile-first ads averaged 20 seconds and were more likely designed for sound off. "The longer the video, the lower the percentage viewed time." Mobile-first outperformed both traditional and adapted ads on recall. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebooks-published-a-new-study-which-highlights-effective-mobile-video-te/512753)
- Aggregator claims: mobile-optimised campaigns deliver 1.9x the ROI of non-optimised assets; sound-off-ready creative gives a 4.8x improvement; 85% of Facebook videos are watched without sound; and a 2016 analysis of 10 advertisers found vertical video performed better on ad recall. — [Collective Measures](https://www.collectivemeasures.com/?p=4389); [AdStellar](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/vertical-video-ad-best-practices) (secondary; primary Meta sources not verified)

### Inferences
- For Reels/Stories-style short video: put the brand on screen within the first 2–3 seconds, make the story legible with sound off (captions or visual storytelling), keep it short (under about 20 seconds), and shoot or crop for 9:16.
- Sound-off design partly conflicts with YouTube's "sound-on" stance and TikTok's "sound matters" finding. Build creative that works without sound but is rewarded with it.

### Gaps
- The 1.9x / 4.8x figures and the 85% sound-off statistic could not be traced to a primary Meta document in this pass, and the 85% figure dates from about 2016. No recent (2022–2026) primary Meta Marketing Science study on the first 3 seconds was retrieved.

## TikTok Creative Center / TikTok Marketing Science

### Takeaway
TikTok's research says ads made natively for TikTok, especially with creators, outperform repurposed ads: about 27% higher ad recall and up to 193% higher 6-second view-through rate. Its best-practice data favours full-screen 9:16, sound on, on-screen text/CTA, a strong 3-second hook, and roughly 21–34 second lengths. Most specific percentages reach the web through third-party blogs.

### Cited Findings
- Ads made for TikTok with creators deliver 27% higher ad recall, up to 193% higher 6-second VTR on auction in-feed, and a 24-point lift in "watching to the end" for beauty ads that show product use or comedy cues. Method: meta-analysis of first-party TikTok data plus third-party surveys. — [Segwise summary](https://segwise.ai/blog/tiktok-creative-analysis-creator-partnerships); [TikTok Works: Recut, Remix, Reimagine (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_Works_Recut_Remix_Reimagine.pdf)
- TikTok's own creative best-practices blog on top-performing ads (I could not fetch it). — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads/)
- Widely cited TikTok-attributed figures: 71% of users decide whether to keep watching in the first 3 seconds; vertical videos get 40.1% more impressions than square/horizontal; full 9:16 sees a 60.5% impression lift over ill-fitting crops; 93% of top-performing videos use sound; a CTA in captions or on-screen text gives a 55.7% impression lift; 21–34 second videos see a 1.6% impression lift. — [Sovran](https://sovran.ai/blog/tiktok-creative-best-practices); [Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/blog/paid-social/tiktok-best-practices/) (secondary)
- EyeSee ran third-party "TikTok creative that works" research with 3M Scotch-Brite and TikTok. — [Quirk's](https://www.quirks.com/articles/eyesee-virtual-session-tiktok-creative-that-works-insights-from-eyesee-3m-scotch-brite-and-tiktok)

### Inferences
- The outcome metrics are impressions and view-through, which TikTok's own delivery algorithm partly determines. "Lift in impressions" reflects how the platform rewards the format, not necessarily persuasion. That is still practically relevant for buying efficiency.
- Recreate creative for each platform rather than resizing TV or YouTube assets. Native creator-style footage, trends and sounds are the platform's stated levers.

### Gaps
- I could not access the primary TikTok Creative Center "top creative codes" or trend-led findings pages (the fetch failed). Exact dates and sample sizes for the 40.1% / 60.5% / 55.7% figures are unverified. They trace to roughly 2021–22 TikTok materials via blogs.

## Kantar LINK and creative-effectiveness findings (2024/2025)

### Takeaway
From more than 13,000 ads across 75 markets, Kantar finds that humour and emotion (surprise, relatability), product demonstration (+10 points persuasion), consistent distinctive assets, and tight music-synced editing in short formats drive effectiveness. In 2025 it highlighted four creative trends: relatable realities, bizarrely bold, roots reimagined, and trend tapping.

### Cited Findings
- 13,000+ ads tested across 75 markets inform the best-practice analysis and Kantar's Creative Effectiveness Awards. — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/inspiration/advertising-media/best-practices-for-creative-effectiveness)
- Humour is "the most powerful enhancer of ad receptivity across all generations". Showing product benefits gives +10 points in persuasion, and showing the product in use or being consumed gives +10 points in brand affinity. Tight editing synced to music helps short formats, and breaking the fourth wall (direct address) boosts engagement. Celebrity used consistently, music and visual brand cues/slogans act as distinctive assets. An Audi 60-to-30-second recut improved brand impact. — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/inspiration/advertising-media/best-practices-for-creative-effectiveness)
- 2025 awards trends: relatable realities (Coca-Cola), bizarrely bold (Deliveroo's half-man, half-octopus ad, the most effective TV ad), roots reimagined (VW), trend tapping (Tourism Australia and Taylor Swift's Eras tour). LINK+ is "independently validated" to predict short-term sales and future brand demand, and creatively effective ads are claimed to be four times more likely to see increased sales and profit ROI. — [More About Advertising](https://www.moreaboutadvertising.com/news/let-ai-be-the-judge-kantars-creative-effectiveness-awards-2025/); [Kantar Creative Effective](https://www.kantar.com/creative-effective); [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2025/04/09/kantar-awards-deliveroo-most-effective-tv-ad-of-the-year/)
- Kantar's Link AI was the measurement engine for Google's ABCD validation (11,000 ads, 180 features). — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence)

### Inferences
- Kantar, System1 and Google agree on emotion and humour plus integrated branding. Kantar adds product demonstration as a persuasion driver. For short ads, product-in-use moments are a strong default.
- The "4x more likely" claim is Kantar validating its own tool. The awards are judged by Kantar's AI and its pre-testing metrics, not by in-market results.

### Gaps
- I did not find Kantar's underlying "meaningfully different / salience" (MDS) validation statistics or a 2024 "what makes ads effective" report with numbers. The "independent validation" of LINK+ is asserted, but the validating party and method were not visible.

## Neuromarketing methods (EEG, fMRI, biometrics, facial coding): what is reliable vs hype

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed evidence shows some neural measures add predictive value. In the ARF-funded Venkatraman et al. (2015), fMRI ventral-striatum activity was the best predictor of market-level ad elasticity beyond self-report. But vendor measures disagree with each other, reverse inference is a fundamental problem, and EEG/biometric "engagement" metrics are noisy. Commercial claims usually outrun the science.

### Cited Findings
- Venkatraman, Dimoka, Pavlou, Vo, Hampton, Bollinger, Hershfield, Ishihara & Winer (2015), Journal of Marketing Research 52(4): 436–452, ARF-funded. They tested 30-second TV ads across six methods (self-report, implicit measures, eye tracking, biometrics, EEG, fMRI). fMRI ventral-striatum activation was the strongest predictor of market-level advertising elasticity beyond traditional measures, and fMRI explained the most added variance. — [Stanford PDF](https://stanford.edu/~knutson/nfc/venkatraman15.pdf); [Temple Fox](https://www3.fox.temple.edu/discover/?p=169184)
- Varan, Lang, Barwise, Weber & Bellman (2015), Journal of Advertising Research 55(2): 176–191, analysed the ARF "Neuro 1" vendor trial. Vendors offer confusing, proprietary methods, and the results showed "no common truth, no single scientific reality" across them. The paper was named JAR Best Academic Paper 2015. — [Ewha/Pure](https://pure.ewha.ac.kr/en/publications/how-reliable-are-neuromarketers-measures-of-advertising-effective/); [LBS](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/100)
- Reverse inference (inferring a specific mental state from regional activation) is an unresolved problem in consumer neuroscience. Ad stimuli cannot be repeated enough to average out noise, because people habituate. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.550204/epub); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7674591/)
- EEG is the most used ad-testing method because it is cheap and has high temporal resolution. But EEG arousal is positively linked to ads being noticed and may be *negatively* linked to attitude toward the ad. — [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2020.00736/epub)
- Validity can be improved by triangulating careful design, meta-analysis, and psychometric and behavioural data alongside neuroimaging. — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7674591/)
- Popular critique: "Why Neuromarketing Is A Neuroscam" (Popular Science, 2013). — [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/science/article/2013-07/why-neuromarketing-neuroscam/); see also the Aston review "This is your brain on neuromarketing: reflections on a decade of research". — [Aston](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/this-is-your-brain-on-neuromarketing-reflections-on-a-decade-of-r)

### Inferences
- Reliable enough to act on: moment-by-moment attention and eye-tracking data (where people look, whether they see the brand), and aggregate emotional-response trends. These converge with survey-based frameworks: emotion, early and integrated branding, and focus.
- Hype: single-number "brain engagement" scores, claims to read specific emotions from regional activation, and proprietary indices with no published validation. Venkatraman's fMRI result came from a lab setup few brands can afford, and it predicts elasticity, not individual creative choices.
- Facial coding (used by System1, Ipsos and others) is scalable but measures expression, not felt emotion. Weight it as one signal.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a 2022–2026 systematic review or meta-analysis quantifying how well neuromarketing measures predict sales. Specific peer-reviewed validity data for facial-coding or online emotion-test products was not found. Published independent tests of System1's or Ipsos's facial-coding predictive validity were not located.
