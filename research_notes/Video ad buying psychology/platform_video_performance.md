# Platform Video Performance: What Gets Views, Retention and Conversions (YouTube/Shorts, X, TikTok, Instagram Reels, LinkedIn)

Source-quality legend used throughout:
- [PLATFORM] = the platform's own first-party data. Large samples, but self-interested and rarely published with full methodology.
- [VENDOR-PLATFORM] = a video-hosting vendor's aggregate customer data (Wistia, Vidyard). Real behavioural data, but skewed to B2B websites and the vendor's customers. Not social feeds.
- [VENDOR-SURVEY] = self-reported survey data. Weakest.
- [AGGREGATOR] = a blog or agency restating stats, often with no traceable primary source. Treat as directional only.
- [PEER-REVIEWED] = academic, peer-reviewed.

## Ideal length and aspect ratio per platform (platform recommendations vs observed data)

### Takeaway
Use 9:16 vertical for TikTok, Reels, Shorts and X vertical video. Platform guidance converges on 15–35s for paid short-form: TikTok says 21–34s, LinkedIn says 15–30s. Completion falls steeply with length on every dataset found. Off-feed hosted video (Wistia) shows the reverse for conversion: longer videos convert better, because they are watched by self-selected, high-intent viewers.

### Cited Findings
**TikTok**
- TikTok's Creative Center recommends 21–34s for In-Feed ads, 9:16, with a 60s maximum for that ad spec — [Cometly summary of TikTok guidance](https://www.cometly.com/post/how-long-should-a-tiktok-ad-be); TikTok's own tips PDF: [ads.tiktok.com 7 Top Creative Tips](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/7TopCreativeTips.pdf). [PLATFORM, via aggregator]
- A widely repeated claim: 21–34s ads see "280% higher conversion rates" than ads under 10s. I could not trace it to a TikTok primary document, so treat it as unverified — [Cometly](https://www.cometly.com/post/how-long-should-a-tiktok-ad-be). [AGGREGATOR]

**YouTube Shorts / long-form**
- Shorts can be up to 3 minutes (previously 60s), effective 15 Oct 2024, for square or vertical aspect ratios — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-expands-shorts-duration-to-3-minutes/); [Mezha](https://mezha.ua/en/2024/10/04/youtube-will-allow-you-to-upload-shorts-up-to-3-minutes-long/). [PLATFORM announcement, reported]
- Creator-tool retention benchmarks for Shorts: above 65% average retention for videos under 15s (above 80% is "exceptional"); above 30% completion is strong for 30–60s Shorts; if 30–40%+ swipe away instantly, the hook is failing — [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate-benchmarks); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-low-watch-time-boost). [AGGREGATOR. YouTube does not publish official benchmarks.]

**LinkedIn**
- LinkedIn recommends 15–30s video ads, to qualify for all Feed and Audience Network placements; ~15s is recommended for ad creative — [Storykit, citing LinkedIn guidance](https://storykit.io/blog/linkedin-video-ads-best-practices); [LinkedIn video ad specs](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/85306). [PLATFORM, via aggregator]
- Completion by length on LinkedIn: under 15s 35–45%; 15–30s 25–35%; 30–60s 15–25%; 1–3 min 8–15%; over 3 min under 8% — [Factors.ai / ZenABM benchmarks](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-video-ads-benchmarks). [AGGREGATOR / agency data; methodology unclear]

**X/Twitter**
- Best-practice guidance commonly cites under 15s so mobile users finish the video — [Biteable](https://biteable.com/blog/best-practices-short-social-video/). [AGGREGATOR. I found no current X primary doc.]
- X's full-screen Vertical Video Ads (9:16, sound-on surface) are open to all advertisers — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-makes-vertical-video-feed-ads-available-all-advertisers/706331/). [PLATFORM, reported]

**Instagram Reels / Meta**
- Meta's tested "Reels-native" recipe combines: 9:16, sound on, kept within safe zones, plus at least one extra element (human presence, text overlay, voiceover, lo-fi style or an early hook). It reported 20% lower CPC, 56% higher CTR, 93% higher ThruPlay rate and 30% higher 3s play rate across placements — [Website Builder Expert, reporting Meta's Reels ads guide](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/news/meta-updates-reels-ads-guide/); [Logical Position](https://www.logicalposition.com/?p=6689). [PLATFORM]
- Ad sets with a 9:16 sound-on video asset had 4.8% lower CPA, 5.1% higher CTR and 2.9% higher conversion rate than other video types — [Booyah, citing Meta](https://www.booyah.com/blog/why-you-should-use-creative-designed-specifically-for-reels/). [PLATFORM]
- "Creating videos in the language of Reels" can improve cost per incremental conversion for lower-funnel events (Purchase, Subscribe) by up to 51% — [Logical Position, citing Meta](https://www.logicalposition.com/?p=6689). [PLATFORM; "up to" is a best case]

**Hosted / website video (cross-platform baseline)**
- Wistia 2025 State of Video (2024 data; 100M+ videos analysed plus a survey of 1,300+ professionals). Average engagement is 47% for videos under 5 min (down 10% year on year), 38% for 5–30 min and 21% for over 30 min. Another summary splits it as under 1 min = 50%, 1–3 min = 46%, 3–5 min = 45% — [Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/wistia-state-of-video-report-engagement-down-content-under-promoted); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/wistia-brand-video-creation-trends-report-2025/743641). [VENDOR-PLATFORM for engagement; VENDOR-SURVEY for production stats]
- Wistia conversion by length is inverted: over 30 min = 17%, 3–5 min = 5%, under 3 min = 2% — [Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/wistia-state-of-video-report-engagement-down-content-under-promoted). [VENDOR-PLATFORM; heavy selection bias, since webinar-style viewers are already high intent]
- Vidyard (2022 data, 1.78M business videos): 53% of viewers complete business videos overall. Completion is 66% under 1 min, 56% at 1–2 min, 50% at 2–10 min, 39% at 10–20 min and 22% over 20 min — [MarketingProfs](https://marketingprofs.com/charts/2023/49949/business-video-benchmarks-retention-rates-by-length). [VENDOR-PLATFORM]

### Inferences
- For a small SaaS brand: cut one master into a 9:16 cut at roughly 20–35s for TikTok, Reels and Shorts paid, a 15–30s cut for LinkedIn and X, and a longer 2–5 min demo for YouTube and the website, where intent is higher.
- Short wins on completion and cheap reach. Long wins on conversion once the viewer is already interested. These are two different jobs, not a contradiction.

### Gaps
- I found no official YouTube, Instagram or X numeric benchmarks for average retention by length. All Shorts and Reels retention benchmarks above come from creator-tool vendors.
- The TikTok "280%" and the frequently cited "67%/63% of top ads deliver key message in first 3s" could not be traced to a dated TikTok primary document in this pass.

## Hook types that work best (question, bold claim, pattern interrupt, problem-first, result-first, text hook)

### Takeaway
Strong first-party evidence says hooking in the first 1–3 seconds matters, and so do early branding and early key messaging. Rigorous comparative data on specific hook types (question vs bold claim vs result-first) is essentially absent from public sources.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok counts an ad view at 3s. Commonly cited TikTok guidance: 63–67% of top-performing ads deliver the key or brand message in the first 3 seconds — [Megadigital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/tiktok-ad-creative/); [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/tiktok-ad-best-practices-improve-performance). [PLATFORM claim via AGGREGATOR]
- "Ads that hook within 2 seconds see 30–40% higher completion" — [Megadigital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/tiktok-ad-creative/). [AGGREGATOR, unsourced; weak]
- Google's ABCD framework (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction) was developed with Ipsos, with Nielsen and Kantar as reviewers. Kantar validated it on 11,000 ads across 180 creative features. Ads following the ABCDs show a 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and a 17% lift in long-term brand contribution. "A = Attention: hook the viewer" from the first moments — [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/advertising-channels/video/youtube-video-ad-creative); [Kantar case study](https://www.kantar.com/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence). [PLATFORM + independent validator; strongest hook/creative evidence found]
- LinkedIn Creative Lab: a visible logo in the first 2 seconds gives a 17% lift in CTR — [Storykit, citing LinkedIn](https://storykit.io/blog/linkedin-video-ads-best-practices). [PLATFORM via aggregator]
- Meta lists "a hook in the first few seconds" and text overlays among the elements in its recipe that showed +30% 3s play rate — [Website Builder Expert](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/news/meta-updates-reels-ads-guide/). [PLATFORM]

### Inferences
- In practice, for SaaS: lead with the problem or the result in the first 1–2s, burn it in as on-screen text (sound-off safety), and show brand or product early. Early branding helps on LinkedIn and YouTube. On TikTok, an ad-looking opening may hurt (see UGC section), so brand the product visually rather than with a logo slate.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous public study comparing hook archetypes (question vs bold claim vs pattern interrupt vs result-first). Claims online are anecdotal or from creative-analytics vendors with undisclosed samples. Recommend A/B testing hooks on one fixed body.

## UGC/creator-style vs polished brand ads

### Takeaway
TikTok's first-party data strongly favours creator-led content (+70% CTR at the same CPM), and Kantar finds people watch influencer ads more than twice as long. It is self-interested but consistent across sources.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok campaign data (Feb 2024–Jan 2025): creator-led ads had 70% higher CTR and 159% higher engagement rate than non-creator ads at the same CPM. Ads posted to creator accounts had 59% higher engagement and 16% higher 6s view-through rate than those posted to advertiser accounts. Case study: Felix Pago saw +38% conversions and +26% CTR — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/tiktok-report-shows-creator-led-ads-drive-70-percent-higher-click-through-rates/). [PLATFORM]
- TikTok-made ads with a creator partnership: 91% higher 6s VTR than TikTok-made ads without one — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/tiktok-creative-analysis-creator-partnerships). [PLATFORM via aggregator]
- Kantar: viewers watch influencer ads for an average of 17.8s before skipping, vs 7.9s for traditional branded content, with 1.4x visibility duration — cited in [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/tiktok-report-shows-creator-led-ads-drive-70-percent-higher-click-through-rates/) / [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/tiktok-creative-analysis-creator-partnerships). [Third-party research, reported second-hand]
- Realeyes attention research: TikTok UGC and brand videos outperform other ad formats in driving attention on mobile — [Realeyes blog](https://blog.realeyesit.com/tiktok-ugc-and-brand-videos-outperform-other-ad-formats-in-driving-attention-in-mobile). [Vendor research]
- Meta's Reels recipe explicitly lists "lo-fi content" and "human presence" as elements that lift performance — [Website Builder Expert](https://www.websitebuilderexpert.com/news/meta-updates-reels-ads-guide/). [PLATFORM]

### Inferences
- A small SaaS brand should default to a founder- or creator-style lo-fi talking head plus screen recording for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, and reserve polish for YouTube pre-roll or website hero video.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, peer-reviewed head-to-head of UGC vs polished ads on conversion (as opposed to CTR or attention). Note the incentive: TikTok sells Creator Marketplace and Spark Ads.

## Video formats: explainer vs product demo vs motion graphics vs talking head vs screen recording

### Takeaway
The only hard format data found is Wistia's: how-to videos have the highest engagement, 82% for under 1 min. Strong public comparative data on views and conversions by format is missing.

### Cited Findings
- Wistia 2025: how-to videos have the highest engagement, at 82% for under-1-min how-tos and 58% for 5–30 min — [Social Media Today / Wistia summary](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/wistia-brand-video-creation-trends-report-2025/743641). [VENDOR-PLATFORM]
- Wistia: video on contact pages gets 51% engagement and on thank-you pages 55%, both higher than landing, product or case-study pages — [Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/wistia-state-of-video-report-engagement-down-content-under-promoted). [VENDOR-PLATFORM]
- Vidyard: website conversion rises from 2.9% to 4.8% with video present — [MarketingProfs summary via search](https://marketingprofs.com/charts/2023/49949/business-video-benchmarks-retention-rates-by-length). [VENDOR; correlational]

### Inferences
- For SaaS: short how-to and product-demo clips (screen recording with a face or voiceover) are likely the best fit for both engagement and intent. Motion graphics suit explainers on YouTube or the website more than TikTok, where native-looking content wins.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable cross-platform data ranking explainer vs demo vs motion graphics vs talking head on views or conversion. HubSpot and Wyzowl publish survey-based claims (e.g. "explainer videos are the most-made type"). These are [VENDOR-SURVEY], measure what marketers report rather than performance, and were not pulled in this pass.

## Retention curves: drop-off shapes and benchmarks

### Takeaway
Every dataset shows the same shape: a steep cliff in the first 1–3 seconds (or first 30s on long-form), then a gradual decay, and completion falling steadily with length. Benchmarks: about 66% complete under-1-min business videos (Vidyard); 35–45% complete under-15s LinkedIn ads; Shorts under 15s should retain above 65%.

### Cited Findings
- Vidyard completion by length: 66% (under 1 min), 56% (1–2 min), 50% (2–10 min), 39% (10–20 min), 22% (over 20 min) — [MarketingProfs](https://marketingprofs.com/charts/2023/49949/business-video-benchmarks-retention-rates-by-length). [VENDOR-PLATFORM]
- Wistia reports overall engagement at a four-year low, with the steepest decline (−10%) for 3–5 min videos — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/wistia-brand-video-creation-trends-report-2025/743641). [VENDOR-PLATFORM]
- YouTube's retention graph flags the first 30 seconds as where most drop-off happens. On Shorts, the "viewed vs swiped away" metric is the hook test, with a target of 70%+ "viewed" — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-low-watch-time-boost); [Satura AI](https://saturaai.com/blog/youtube-shorts-swipe-ratio-81-percent-rule). [AGGREGATOR]
- TikTok uses 2s/6s view-through as standard metrics (e.g. +16% 6s VTR for creator accounts) — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/tiktok-report-shows-creator-led-ads-drive-70-percent-higher-click-through-rates/). [PLATFORM]

### Inferences
- Optimise in order: the 3s hold (hook), then the 6s/50% point (the payoff of the hook), then completion (tight edit, loopable endings on Shorts and TikTok).

### Gaps
- I found no official, platform-published "share who watch past 3s / 50%" benchmarks for TikTok, Reels or YouTube. These exist only in private ad-account data.

## CTA placement and type

### Takeaway
Wistia's platform data shows embedded, clickable CTAs and forms convert meaningfully: 16% for clickable CTAs and 24% for lead forms on hosted video. Claims that mid-roll CTAs beat end CTAs come from low-quality aggregators. Google's ABCD "Direction" (a clear CTA) is part of the validated +30% sales-likelihood framework.

### Cited Findings
- Wistia: embedded lead forms have a 24% completion rate; clickable CTAs ("Book a demo", "Download") have a 16% response rate — [Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/wistia-state-of-video-report-engagement-down-content-under-promoted). [VENDOR-PLATFORM]
- "96% of creators put CTAs at the end, but mid-roll CTAs convert at 16.95%, early at 14%." A related claim says CTAs integrated during high-engagement moments convert up to 3x better than end-screen-only CTAs — [Opus Pro blog](https://www.opus.pro/de-de/blog/monetizing-videos-with-strategic-caption-placement-and-cta-integration); [Wistia CTA guide](https://wistia.com/blog/using-video-ctas). [AGGREGATOR; the 16.95% figure traces to older Wistia data and its date is unclear]
- YouTube end screens typically get 3–7% CTR, vs 0.5–3% for cards — [ytshark](https://ytshark.com/?p=263). [AGGREGATOR]
- ABCD's "D = Direction" (a clear CTA) is one of the four validated principles — [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/advertising-channels/video/youtube-video-ad-creative). [PLATFORM + Kantar]

### Inferences
- For short paid social: state the offer or brand early (most viewers leave before the end), repeat the CTA on screen near the end, and rely on the platform's CTA button. For hosted demos: use a mid-video clickable CTA plus an end form.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled public data on on-screen vs spoken CTAs. Early vs late evidence is anecdotal or vendor-blog quality.

## What X/Twitter rewards for video; LinkedIn B2B video benchmarks

### Takeaway
X is pushing a full-screen vertical, sound-on video feed: 7x engagement vs the same ad in the timeline and −14% CPM, per X. The home timeline still autoplays muted, so captions are mandatory there. LinkedIn rewards short (15–30s), captioned, early-branded video. Completion is strongly length-dependent, with large regional variance.

### Cited Findings
- X (2024): 100M users engage with vertical video daily; video is in more than 4 of 5 sessions; vertical video is over 20% of usage. Vertical Video Ads get 7x higher engagement than the same ad in the Home Timeline and about 14% lower CPMs for early adopters — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-makes-vertical-video-feed-ads-available-all-advertisers/706331/). [PLATFORM; X's numbers have limited independent verification]
- Timeline autoplay is muted on most social feeds, so captions and text overlays are essential. Captions are reported to boost view time by ~12% — [Biteable](https://biteable.com/blog/best-practices-short-social-video/). [AGGREGATOR]
- LinkedIn: logo in the first 2s gives +17% CTR. Recommended length is 15–30s. Use burned-in captions or an SRT file because of sound-off viewing — [Storykit](https://storykit.io/blog/linkedin-video-ads-best-practices). [PLATFORM via aggregator]
- LinkedIn video ad engagement is ~1.6%, about 3x non-video formats. 58% of B2B marketers say video delivers their best results — [Factors.ai](https://www.factors.ai/blog/b2b-linkedin-video-ads). [AGGREGATOR / survey]
- LinkedIn completion by region (2025): North America 56.67%, EMEA 20.66%, APAC 1.48%, LATAM 1.34%, global 23.02% — [ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-video-ads-benchmarks). [AGGREGATOR; regional spread looks anomalous, verify before use]

### Inferences
- On X, upload natively in 9:16 and caption everything; do not link out to YouTube.
- On LinkedIn, a 15–30s captioned product demo with the logo in the first 2s is the default.

### Gaps
- I found no current official X statement on algorithmic preference for native video vs links. Engagement claims for native video ("10x engagement for video tweets") are older Twitter-era figures repeated by aggregators.

## Organic vs paid differences, and what makes videos shareable or viral

### Takeaway
High-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) drive sharing; low-arousal sadness suppresses it (peer-reviewed). Paid creative succeeds when it looks organic ("Reels-native", creator-led). Organic success depends on completion, rewatches and shares more than on ad metrics like CTR.

### Cited Findings
- Berger & Milkman (Journal of Marketing Research, 2012; ~7,000 NYT articles over 3 months, plus experiments). High-arousal positive (awe) and negative (anger, anxiety) emotions increase virality; low-arousal emotions (sadness) decrease it. Positive content is more viral than negative overall — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf); [MSI working paper](https://www.msi.org/working-papers/social-transmission-emotion-and-the-virality-of-online-content). [PEER-REVIEWED; pre-2022, but foundational]
- Effect sizes: a one standard deviation increase in anger raises the odds of making the most-emailed list by 34%, and awe by 30% — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral); [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=10141). [PEER-REVIEWED, reported]
- Surprise, interest and practical utility are also positively linked to sharing, and arousal effects hold when controlling for them — [Berger & Milkman via QUBES](https://qubeshub.org/app/site/collections/4299/Berger__Milkman.pdf). [PEER-REVIEWED]
- Paid: native-looking ads outperform ad-looking ads on TikTok — [Megadigital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/tiktok-ad-creative/) [AGGREGATOR]. Meta's Reels-language creative improves cost per incremental conversion by up to 51% — [Logical Position](https://www.logicalposition.com/?p=6689). [PLATFORM]
- Shorts creators treat over 100% retention (rewatches and loops) as a strong distribution signal — [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate-benchmarks). [AGGREGATOR]

### Inferences
- For SaaS: practical utility (how-to, a "this saves 2 hours" demo), awe (an impressive result reveal) and mild frustration with the status quo (problem-first "anger-lite") are the shareable levers. Avoid sad or low-energy framing.
- Run organic first to find winning hooks, then put paid spend behind proven organic posts (Spark Ads, partnership ads).

### Gaps
- The Berger & Milkman data is text news from 2008–2009, not video, and not B2B. I found no 2022–2026 peer-reviewed replication on short-form video virality in this pass. Direct organic-vs-paid performance comparisons were not found.
